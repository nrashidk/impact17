# SDG 13 — Climate Action

## 13.1 — Calculate and reduce your carbon footprint
- **Effort:** Hard | **Points:** 20 | **Verification:** Photo of the written footprint calculation/plan *(rewritten)*
- **How to do this:**
  - Use a carbon footprint calculator to estimate your household's emissions — travel, electricity, food, waste.
  - Identify your three biggest sources.
  - Make specific changes and track them over a few weeks.
  - Record the reduction.
  - Photograph your written calculation and plan.
- **Reflection:** What's your biggest emission source? What change worked? What did you enjoy?

## 13.2 — Plant a tree for carbon capture
- **Effort:** Hard | **Points:** 20 | **Verification:** Photo of the planted tree
- **How to do this:**
  - Choose a native, climate-suited species — Ghaf, Sidr, Date Palm.
  - Plant it in cooler months (October–March) in a permitted spot.
  - Water deeply and mulch.
  - Plan how it'll be cared for long-term — a tree only captures carbon if it survives.
  - Photograph yourself with the planted tree.
- **Reflection:** What did you plant, and where? Who'll care for it? What did you enjoy?

## 13.3 — Switch to low-carbon transport for a week and log it
- **Effort:** Medium | **Points:** 10 | **Verification:** Photo of the transport log *(rewritten)*
- **How to do this:**
  - For a week, choose walking, cycling, metro, bus, or carpooling over a private car where possible.
  - Track each trip and the car journeys you avoided.
  - Note what was practical and what wasn't in the UAE climate and layout.
  - Total your avoided emissions roughly.
  - Photograph your transport log.
- **Reflection:** What was easiest? What was hard? What did you enjoy?

## 13.4 — Run a home energy-reduction project
- **Effort:** Medium | **Points:** 10 | **Verification:** Photo of the changes made *(rewritten)*
- **How to do this:**
  - Target your home's biggest energy use — almost always AC in the UAE.
  - Make real changes: AC at 24°C, seal gaps, use fans, close blinds in the day.
  - Run the changes for two to three weeks.
  - Check the bill or meter for the difference.
  - Photograph the changes you made.
- **Reflection:** What change had the biggest effect? What did you enjoy?

## 13.5 — Make a poster on climate impacts in the UAE
- **Effort:** Easy | **Points:** 5 | **Verification:** Photo of the poster *(rewritten)*
- **How to do this:**
  - Research how climate change affects the UAE specifically — heat, water, coastlines, biodiversity.
  - Pick a few clear, accurate points.
  - Design a poster.
  - Display or share it.
  - Photograph your finished poster.
- **Reflection:** Which impact concerns you most? Where did you share it? What did you enjoy?

## 13.6 — Start a climate-focused habit and track it for a month
- **Effort:** Hard | **Points:** 20 | **Verification:** Photo of the month-long tracker *(rewritten)*
- **How to do this:**
  - Choose one sustained habit — meat-free days, no-car days, strict energy saving.
  - Track it daily for a month.
  - Keep it realistic enough to maintain.
  - Complete the full month.
  - Photograph your completed tracker.
- **Reflection:** What habit did you build? Will you keep it? What did you enjoy?

## 13.7 — Organise a climate awareness session
- **Effort:** Hard | **Points:** 20 | **Verification:** Photo of the session
- **How to do this:**
  - Prepare an accurate, non-alarmist session for classmates or a community group.
  - Focus on both the problem and concrete actions.
  - Get permission for the space and time.
  - Facilitate it so people leave with something they can do.
  - Photograph the session.
- **Reflection:** What did you focus on? What actions did people commit to? What did you enjoy?

## 13.8 — Create a climate-resilient garden
- **Effort:** Medium | **Points:** 10 | **Verification:** Photo of the garden
- **How to do this:**
  - Design a small garden built for the UAE climate — native, drought-tolerant, shaded, water-efficient.
  - Group plants by water need.
  - Plant it and set up efficient watering.
  - Make it genuinely low-input.
  - Photograph your garden.
- **Reflection:** What makes your garden climate-resilient? What did you enjoy?

## 13.9 — Document local weather or climate patterns
- **Effort:** Easy | **Points:** 5 | **Verification:** Photo of the written log/data *(rewritten)*
- **How to do this:**
  - Track local conditions over at least two weeks — temperature, humidity, dust, any rain.
  - Record it consistently.
  - Look for patterns and note them.
  - Write up what you observed.
  - Photograph your written log or data.
- **Reflection:** What pattern did you notice? What did you enjoy?

## 13.10 — Reduce and offset an event's footprint
- **Effort:** Medium | **Points:** 10 | **Verification:** Photo of the event's sustainability measures *(rewritten)*
- **How to do this:**
  - Take an event you're involved in — a party, a school activity, a gathering.
  - Cut its footprint: less waste, reusables, efficient transport, local food.
  - Put the measures in place.
  - Note what was reduced.
  - Photograph the sustainability measures in action.
- **Reflection:** What did you change about the event? What did you enjoy?

# SDG 14 — Life Below Water

## 14.1 — Join or organise a beach or coastal clean-up
- **Effort:** Hard | **Points:** 20 | **Verification:** Photo of the clean-up with collected litter
- **How to do this:**
  - Choose a UAE beach, corniche, or coastal area.
  - Bring gloves, bags, water; go at a cooler time.
  - Collect litter, focusing on plastic before it reaches the water.
  - Sort recyclables; join an organised clean-up if one's running.
  - Photograph yourself with the collected litter.
- **Reflection:** Where did you go? What did you find most of? What did you enjoy?

## 14.2 — Cut single-use plastics that reach the ocean
- **Effort:** Medium | **Points:** 10 | **Verification:** Photo of the reusable swaps *(rewritten)*
- **How to do this:**
  - Identify the single-use plastics you use that commonly end up in oceans — bottles, bags, straws, wrappers.
  - Replace them with reusables.
  - Stick with it for at least a week.
  - Note what you avoided.
  - Photograph your reusable swaps.
- **Reflection:** What was hardest to give up? What did you enjoy?

## 14.3 — Make a poster on the UAE's marine life
- **Effort:** Easy | **Points:** 5 | **Verification:** Photo of the poster *(rewritten)*
- **How to do this:**
  - Research UAE marine life — dugongs, sea turtles, coral, the Arabian Gulf's reefs.
  - Pick a species or ecosystem and a few clear facts.
  - Design a poster.
  - Display or share it.
  - Photograph your finished poster.
- **Reflection:** What marine life did you feature? What surprised you? What did you enjoy?

## 14.4 — Properly dispose of fishing line, nets, or hooks
- **Effort:** Easy | **Points:** 5 | **Verification:** Photo of the collected gear
- **How to do this:**
  - Look for discarded fishing gear near coastal or fishing areas — it's deadly to marine life.
  - Collect it safely (hooks and lines need care).
  - Dispose of it properly.
  - Note where you found it.
  - Photograph the gear you collected.
- **Reflection:** What did you find, and where? Why is it dangerous? What did you enjoy?

## 14.5 — Choose and document sustainable seafood
- **Effort:** Medium | **Points:** 10 | **Verification:** Photo of the sustainable seafood/guide *(rewritten)*
- **How to do this:**
  - Learn which local fish are sustainable and which are overfished in the Gulf.
  - Make a simple guide for your household.
  - Use it for one seafood purchase or meal.
  - Note the choice you made.
  - Photograph the sustainable seafood or your guide.
- **Reflection:** What did you learn about Gulf fish stocks? What did you enjoy?

## 14.6 — Set up a storm drain or waterway awareness marker
- **Effort:** Medium | **Points:** 10 | **Verification:** Photo of the marker/activity *(rewritten)*
- **How to do this:**
  - Learn how local storm drains connect to the sea — litter and chemicals travel straight there.
  - With permission, create an awareness marker or sign near a drain, or make an informational display.
  - Place it where people will see it.
  - Explain the drain-to-sea link clearly.
  - Photograph your marker or display.
- **Reflection:** What did your marker say? Did people notice? What did you enjoy?

## 14.7 — Create marine-themed art from beach waste
- **Effort:** Easy | **Points:** 5 | **Verification:** Photo of the artwork
- **How to do this:**
  - Collect clean waste from a beach clean-up.
  - Turn it into art with a marine conservation message.
  - Complete the piece.
  - Display or share it.
  - Photograph your finished artwork.
- **Reflection:** What did you make, from what? What's the message? What did you enjoy?

## 14.8 — Do a water quality observation
- **Effort:** Medium | **Points:** 10 | **Verification:** Photo of the observation/notes *(rewritten)*
- **How to do this:**
  - Visit a safe, accessible coastal spot.
  - Observe and record water quality indicators — clarity, litter, algae, wildlife presence.
  - Do this consistently if you can repeat visits.
  - Write up what you observed.
  - Photograph your observation notes.
- **Reflection:** What did the water quality tell you? What did you enjoy?

## 14.9 — Run an ocean conservation session
- **Effort:** Hard | **Points:** 20 | **Verification:** Photo of the session
- **How to do this:**
  - Prepare a session on ocean health for classmates or a community group.
  - Cover the Gulf specifically — its marine life, its pressures, what helps.
  - Get permission for space and time.
  - Facilitate it with clear actions people can take.
  - Photograph the session.
- **Reflection:** What did you cover? What did people take away? What did you enjoy?

## 14.10 — Protect a coastal or marine habitat
- **Effort:** Hard | **Points:** 20 | **Verification:** Photo of the protection activity
- **How to do this:**
  - Identify a coastal habitat that needs care — a beach, mangroves, a tidal area.
  - Find a real protective action — clean-up, removing hazards, supporting an organised effort.
  - Do the work responsibly, without disturbing wildlife.
  - Make it count.
  - Photograph the protection activity.
- **Reflection:** What habitat did you help? How? What did you enjoy?

# SDG 16 — Peace, Justice and Strong Institutions

*Note: SDG 16 is the hardest goal in the entire framework to turn into individual photo-verifiable actions. "Strong institutions" is not something an individual does and photographs. Every action here is the "create an artefact / document an activity" pattern. This is a structural limitation of applying a photo-verification model to this goal — flagged honestly, not hidden.*

## 16.1 — Make a guide to your rights and responsibilities
- **Effort:** Easy | **Points:** 5 | **Verification:** Photo of the guide
- **How to do this:**
  - Research the basic rights and responsibilities of a resident or citizen in the UAE.
  - Pick the most important and least understood points.
  - Design a clear, accurate guide.
  - Share it with classmates or family.
  - Photograph your finished guide.
- **Reflection:** What right or responsibility is least understood? What did you enjoy?

## 16.2 — Document a fair conflict resolution
- **Effort:** Medium | **Points:** 10 | **Verification:** Photo of the written reflection
- **How to do this:**
  - When a real disagreement comes up, work towards a fair resolution — listening, compromise, respect.
  - Afterwards, write up how it was resolved and what made it fair.
  - Keep names private.
  - Focus on the process, not the people.
  - Photograph your written reflection.
- **Reflection:** What made the resolution fair? What would you do differently? What did you enjoy?

## 16.3 — Create an anti-bullying or fairness campaign piece
- **Effort:** Easy | **Points:** 5 | **Verification:** Photo of the campaign piece
- **How to do this:**
  - Choose a focus — bullying, fairness, standing up for others.
  - Create a campaign piece — a poster, a video, a presentation.
  - Make the message clear and constructive.
  - Share it where it'll have an effect.
  - Photograph your campaign piece.
- **Reflection:** What's your message? Where did you share it? What did you enjoy?

## 16.4 — Run a mock democratic or decision-making exercise
- **Effort:** Hard | **Points:** 20 | **Verification:** Photo of the exercise
- **How to do this:**
  - Organise a structured group decision — a mock council, a class vote, a debate with a fair process.
  - Set clear, fair rules everyone agrees to.
  - Run it so every voice is heard.
  - Reach a decision through the agreed process.
  - Photograph the exercise.
- **Reflection:** Was the process fair? What did people learn? What did you enjoy?

## 16.5 — Interview someone about justice or public service
- **Effort:** Medium | **Points:** 10 | **Verification:** Photo of the written summary
- **How to do this:**
  - Find someone who works in law, public service, or community leadership.
  - Prepare respectful questions about fairness, service, and institutions.
  - Listen and take notes.
  - Write up what you learned.
  - Photograph your written summary.
- **Reflection:** What did you learn about how institutions work? What did you enjoy?

## 16.6 — Create a transparency or honesty project
- **Effort:** Medium | **Points:** 10 | **Verification:** Photo of the project
- **How to do this:**
  - Pick a context — a club, a class project, an event — and build transparency into it.
  - Make decisions, money, or processes open and documented.
  - Run it that way.
  - Show how transparency was maintained.
  - Photograph your transparency project.
- **Reflection:** How did transparency change things? What did you enjoy?

## 16.7 — Make a guide to a UAE institution and how it serves people
- **Effort:** Easy | **Points:** 5 | **Verification:** Photo of the guide
- **How to do this:**
  - Choose a UAE institution — a court, a municipality, a public service body.
  - Research what it does and how a person accesses it.
  - Create a clear guide.
  - Share it with people who'd find it useful.
  - Photograph your finished guide.
- **Reflection:** What does this institution do that you didn't know? What did you enjoy?

## 16.8 — Organise a peer mediation or listening circle
- **Effort:** Hard | **Points:** 20 | **Verification:** Photo of the activity
- **How to do this:**
  - Set up a structured space where people can resolve issues or be heard fairly.
  - Establish ground rules — respect, confidentiality, equal time.
  - Facilitate it neutrally.
  - Make sure everyone leaves heard.
  - Photograph the activity (no identifying details).
- **Reflection:** Did people feel heard? What made it work? What did you enjoy?

## 16.9 — Write a piece on a justice issue you care about
- **Effort:** Easy | **Points:** 5 | **Verification:** Photo/screenshot of the written piece
- **How to do this:**
  - Choose a justice or fairness issue you genuinely care about.
  - Research it properly and form a thoughtful view.
  - Write a clear, honest piece — an article, an essay, a post.
  - Share it.
  - Photograph or screenshot your published piece.
- **Reflection:** What's your main argument? Where did you share it? What did you enjoy?

## 16.10 — Document an act of civic responsibility
- **Effort:** Medium | **Points:** 10 | **Verification:** Photo of the civic activity
- **How to do this:**
  - Take a real civic action — reporting a public issue, participating in a community process, following a fair procedure properly.
  - Do it thoroughly.
  - Note why it supports a functioning community.
  - Keep a record.
  - Photograph the civic activity or its documentation.
- **Reflection:** What civic action did you take? Why does it matter? What did you enjoy?

**Totals:**
- 170 actions across 17 SDGs
- Action points available: ~1,850
- SDG completion bonuses: 5,100 (17 × 300)
- Full completion (all 170 actions + all 17 bonuses): ~6,950 points
- Roughly 75 of 170 actions rewritten from non-verifiable originals in the source document

**The honest strength distribution:**
- **Strong/strongest (8 SDGs):** 2, 6, 7, 11, 12, 13, 14, 15 — genuinely physical, photographable, UAE-localised actions
- **Medium (3 SDGs):** 3, 4, 9 — mix of physical and artefact actions
- **Soft/artefact-heavy (6 SDGs):** 1, 5, 8, 10, 16, 17 — few photographable individual actions; rely on "make a poster / write a guide / document an activity"

**This distribution is the structural consequence of the all-17-SDGs decision.** Roughly a third of the catalogue can't be meaningfully photo-verified because the underlying goals aren't individually actionable in a physical way. The photo verification confirms an artefact exists, not that impact occurred. This was flagged before drafting and is documented here so it's a known, accepted property of the product — not a surprise discovered after launch.
